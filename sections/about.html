My hands trembled slightly as I connected the final IoT module to our fire detection system. This was not a lab assignment; it was a system that would be installed in Dhaka's most densely populated slums, where fires spread within minutes and often claim lives.

When the residents gathered to watch our demonstration, their applause meant more than any academic recognition I'd ever received. That moment crystallized everything I believe about engineering: it's not about the complexity of the code or the elegance of the algorithm—it's about the real impact on real people.

I'm Firoz Wadud, and I see myself as a translator between two worlds: the cutting-edge realm of robotics and AI research, and the everyday challenges that need solving. Whether it's building Mars rovers that push the boundaries of autonomous exploration or developing agricultural robots that help farmers feed their communities, I'm driven by the same question: "How can this technology make someone's life better?"

My Vision

I dream of a world where robots work alongside humans, not replacing them, but amplifying their capabilities. Where farmers in rural Bangladesh have access to the same precision agriculture tools as those in Silicon Valley. Where technology serves humanity's needs rather than dictating them.

This isn't just idealism—it's a practical roadmap. Every line of code I write, every robot I build, every team I collaborate with brings us closer to this vision.

Engineering Principles

Build for people first, technology second—understand the human context before writing a single line of code

Embrace complexity, but deliver simplicity—the best interfaces hide the hardest engineering challenges

Fail forward, not backward—every setback carries the seeds of the next breakthrough

Dream big, start small, iterate fast—every Mars rover begins with a first step
